Data & Intelligence Lab

We study big data, artificial intelligence, and intelligent systems. We value collaboration through funded research, industry partnerships, and student projects.

Research

AI & Information Retrieval

  • LLMs, RAG, and agentic information retrieval

    We study large language models as retrieval agents rather than just generators. The work combines dataset discovery, metadata augmentation, hybrid search, and GraphRAG-style provenance so answers can be traced back to evidence. We are also interested in how these systems plan retrieval steps and handle complex, weakly described data resources.

  • Trustworthy LLM-in-the-middle for Physical AI

    We explore how LLMs and multimodal foundation models can sit between perception, planning, and control in safety-critical settings. The focus is on semantic reasoning, uncertainty-aware decision support, and runtime safety supervision. We also study hallucination mitigation, traceability, and causal reasoning in real environments.

  • AI interpretability

    We work on interpretable machine learning for systems that need to be understood, audited, and trusted. That includes feature attribution, model diagnostics, and explanations that remain useful when data changes over time. The goal is to pair predictive performance with transparent decisions that can support real operational use.

  • Multimodal AI and environmental monitoring

    This area brings together Earth observation, sensor fusion, and digital-twin style analysis. We study image segmentation, anomaly detection, and multimodal models for monitoring environmental change. The broader goal is to turn heterogeneous data streams into actionable insight for field, infrastructure, and climate-related contexts.

  • NLP and semantic password forensics

    We study NLP methods for password analysis and related security problems. Word embeddings, similarity search, and semantic retrieval can reveal structure in user-generated secrets and support password guessing research. The broader theme is how language technology can be repurposed for security analysis and forensic inspection.

  • Digital health and wearable sensing

    We work with mobile and wearable data in rehabilitation, activity tracking, and stress monitoring. The research focuses on models that can learn from noisy, real-world physiological signals and patient-generated data. We are especially interested in adaptive software that can support health decisions over time.
